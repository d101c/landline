Landline — your iPhone's calls, messages and notifications on the GNOME desktop.

A quiet dot in the top bar when the iPhone is linked over Bluetooth. When something arrives the dot grows into a pill: read and answer an SMS or iMessage from a small card, answer or decline a call and choose whether its sound plays on the computer or on the iPhone. Notifications of the other iPhone apps appear in the GNOME notification list. The menu also shows the battery level, recent calls, and can switch on the iPhone's Personal Hotspot connection. Nothing is installed on the iPhone and no account or cloud service is involved.

REQUIRES A SEPARATE ENGINE. The extension is only the interface. The Bluetooth work is done by a small background service, the Landline engine, which is not part of this extension and must be installed first. Instructions: https://github.com/d101c/landline#install-the-engine
Without the engine the extension shows "Engine not running" and does nothing else.

What the extension does on your system:
- Processes it starts: "systemctl --user start landline-engine.service" once when the engine does not answer; "systemctl --user restart landline-engine.service" when you click "Fix It"; and the read-only "status" command of the engine's setup helper (landline-setup, when installed), to know whether the Bluetooth settings are in place.
- Administrator password: only on systems where a Bluetooth setting required by iPhones is missing (BlueZ older than 5.88 needs its experimental mode; some systems disable L2CAP ERTM). The setup dialog then offers to change it ("pkexec /usr/local/libexec/landline/landline-setup apply"), and the "Undo" button of Phone Settings puts it back ("pkexec /usr/local/libexec/landline/landline-setup revert"). In both cases the password window is the system's own (pkexec), and it is only ever used on the setup helper that the engine's installer placed in that root-owned directory, after checking that the file belongs to root and that nobody else can write it. If that helper is not installed, no password is asked: the dialog shows the commands to run yourself, and Phone Settings has no "Undo" button.
- Clipboard: the "Copy Commands" button writes those commands to the clipboard when you click it. The extension never reads the clipboard.
- Links: "Reply in app" on a WhatsApp, Telegram or Signal notification opens that app's own link (web.whatsapp.com, tg:, sgnl:) with the sender's phone number, on your click.
- Network: no network request. "Use iPhone's Internet Connection" asks NetworkManager to turn the iPhone's existing Bluetooth profile on or off.
- No telemetry.

Limits:
- iPhone only. Text replies work for SMS and iMessage; for other apps the extension shows the notification and can open the app, but cannot send a reply itself.
- Photos and other attachments are not shown, group conversations cannot be started from the computer.
- The extension is inactive on the lock screen.
- Tested on GNOME 51 with BlueZ 5.87 and PipeWire.
